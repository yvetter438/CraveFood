Link-in-bio vs. a Crave page

Your bio link is prime real estate. A traditional link-in-bio page and a Crave page solve different problems—here's how to think about which one belongs in your bio.

July 4, 2026 By Crave Product

If you make recipe content, you've probably used a link-in-bio tool—a single page with buttons for your cookbook, Amazon storefront, newsletter, and whatever else you're promoting. It works. It's familiar. Fans know what to do.

A Crave page is built for a different moment: someone taps your link right after watching you cook. They're still thinking about the dish. They want that pan, that sauce, those noodles—not a menu of ten unrelated links.

This isn't an either/or debate about ditching your current setup. It's about matching the link to the intent behind the tap.

What a link-in-bio page is good at

Link-in-bio tools are general-purpose. One URL, many destinations. That flexibility is the whole point.

  • Everything in one place — Shop, subscribe, book a consult, listen to your podcast.
  • Low setup — Add links, reorder buttons, done.
  • Platform-agnostic — Same page works whether traffic comes from Instagram, TikTok, or email.

For creators who wear a lot of hats, that's hard to beat. If your audience might want any of five different things when they land on your page, a link list is the right shape.

Where link-in-bio falls short for recipe creators

Recipe audiences often arrive with one job: I just watched that video—how do I make it (or buy what they used)? A vertical list of links doesn't answer that directly.

  • Context gets lost. Your latest Reel was chicken tikka masala. Your bio link opens to the same static page as last month's pasta video. The fan has to hunt.
  • Shopping is below the fold. Affiliate links for ingredients sit alongside merch, Patreon, and your Substack. High-intent shoppers scroll past noise.
  • Video and links live in separate tabs. Watch on Instagram, shop on a generic link page, hope they remember which product you meant.

None of this means link-in-bio is bad. It means it wasn't designed for shoppable recipe video—the format most food creators are growing on right now.

What a Crave page does differently

Crave is a video-first page at crave.food/yourname. Fans land on a profile that looks like your Reels or TikTok grid, pick the recipe they just watched, and shop while the video plays.

  • Video-first profile — A grid of your posts so fans can jump straight to the recipe they care about.
  • Timed ingredient pops — Shoppable cards appear when each ingredient is on screen, so the link shows up at the moment of highest intent.
  • Full ingredient list — View every product tied to that video and tap Shop on each one.
  • Your affiliate links — Amazon, DTC, or any partner URL per item. You keep 100% of commissions; Crave charges for the tool.
  • Built for in-app browsers — Optimized for the Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube browsers where link-in-bio traffic actually lands.

The flow is: tap bio link → find the video → watch → shop what you saw. Not: tap bio link → scroll a list → guess which link matches the video you closed thirty seconds ago.

Side by side

  • Best for many unrelated offers — Link-in-bio
  • Best for shoppable recipe video — Crave
  • Fan already watched a specific dish — Crave
  • Fan wants your podcast, shop, and newsletter — Link-in-bio
  • Affiliate revenue from ingredients and tools — Crave
  • Five-minute setup, any niche — Link-in-bio

Which should go in your bio?

If recipe video is how you grow and how you earn, your bio link should get fans into that experience—not away from it. That's the case for putting a Crave page in your bio when shopping is a core part of your business.

Many creators will still want a general link-in-bio somewhere: pinned comment, YouTube description, or a secondary link on platforms that allow more than one. Use Crave for the traffic that comes straight from recipe content. Use link-in-bio for everything else.

A simple rule: if they came from a video about food, send them to a page built for food video.
